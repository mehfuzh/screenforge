What it does

Record, edit, export.
One native app.

A recorder and a timeline editor in a single window. No Electron, no web view; the compositor runs on the GPU and the UI is drawn natively.

AI editing agent new

Edit by asking

Tell the agent what you want, like cut the ums or zoom where I click, and watch the timeline change as it works. Auto edit tightens the whole video; one ⌘Z undoes a whole answer.

Any video

Edit any MP4 or MOV

Drop a video (.mp4, .mov, .m4v) on ScreenForge to start a project, or on an open project to add it to the timeline, then edit by hand or ask the agent. Screen Studio projects open too.

Zoom

Automatic zooms

Zooms open on your clicks and follow the cursor as you work, easing in and out. Adjust or remove any of them on the timeline.

Audio

Split your voice from the video

Your voice lives on its own track. Split, trim, move and mute it separately from the video.

Capture

Native capture

ScreenCaptureKit under the hood. Record a display, a window or an area, with your webcam alongside; click a window to pick it.

Camera

You on camera

Put your webcam over the screen, side by side with it, or full screen for a talking-head video, and switch layouts along the timeline. The camera follows your face, and Resolve-style color grading makes it look right.

iPhone and iPad

Record your apps

Plug in an iPhone or iPad and record its screen, then show it in a phone or tablet frame. On macOS.

Render

GPU rendering and export

A wgpu compositor renders the timeline. Export MP4 (H.264 / HEVC), ProRes, GIF or WebM, with mixed audio.

Captions

Captions from your voice

Transcribed on your computer with Whisper, nothing uploaded. Line by line or word by word, burned into the export or saved as SRT.

Privacy

Blur and highlight masks

Blur passwords, emails and other private details, or dim everything except the part that matters. Draw the areas on the preview and time them on the timeline.

Framing

Crop

Trim the recording to the part of the screen you need. Drag the frame over the whole recording; zooms, cursor and masks follow the crop.

Keyboard

Shortcuts on screen

The shortcuts you press while recording show up as key caps, like ⇧ ⌘ S, so viewers can follow along. Plain key presses too, if you want them.

Voice-over

Narrate after you record

Press R and talk over the video as it plays from the playhead. Each take lands on its own lane: move it, trim its edges, split out the ums, set its volume. It stays with its frame when you cut or speed up the clips before it. No mic? Type a script and an AI voice reads it, made on your computer, with captions from the script.
