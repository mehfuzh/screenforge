Install

  1. Step 1Open the DMGDouble-click the file you just downloaded. A window opens with the app and a shortcut to Applications.

Install

  1. Step 1Install the packageFrom the folder you downloaded to, run sudo apt install ./screenforge_amd64.deb, or open it in your software center. ffmpeg comes bundled; nothing else to install.
  2. Step 2Share your screenRecording works on X11 and Wayland. On Wayland (GNOME, KDE Plasma) your first recording opens the system's "Share your screen" dialog: pick your screen and click Share. ScreenForge remembers the choice and only asks again for a different monitor. To record a window, pick it on the dialog's Window tab. On Wayland the cursor is part of the video and clicks and keystrokes are not recorded, so automatic zooms and cursor effects are not available there yet.
  3. Step 3LaunchOpen ScreenForge from your app menu, or run screenforge in a terminal. System audio recording is not available on Linux yet; the microphone works.
$ sudo apt install ./screenforge_amd64.deb   # Debian / Ubuntu
$ tar xzf ScreenForge-linux-x86_64.tar.gz && ./ScreenForge/screenforge   # other distros
$ systemctl --user restart xdg-desktop-portal   # Wayland: no "Share your screen" dialog? restart the portal

The app runs without a license. Recording and editing are free; exporting needs a key from the pricing page.

  2. Step 2RecordNo permissions to grant: the screen is captured through the Desktop Duplication API, and the cursor, clicks and keystrokes are recorded separately so the editor can smooth and zoom them. System audio and the microphone both work.
